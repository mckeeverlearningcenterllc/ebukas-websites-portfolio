Can You Accept Card and Bank Transfer Payments Without a Website?

By Jonabby & Co. Enterprises · November 7, 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer: yes, you genuinely can — and it's worth being upfront about that rather than pretending you need a full website just to take a card payment. The more useful question is when a standalone payment link is enough, and when it starts holding your business back.

What "accepting payment online" actually requires

At the technical core, it just needs a payment gateway account — Paystack or Flutterwave, both built for the Nigerian market — connected to your bank account. That gateway is what actually processes the card or transfer, verifies it, and moves the money. A website is one way to connect to it. It isn't the only way.

Payment links: the honest shortcut

Both Paystack and Flutterwave let you generate a standalone payment link or page for a specific amount, with no website required — you can drop that link straight into a WhatsApp chat and the customer pays through it directly. For a service business, a one-off sale, or someone just starting out, this is a completely legitimate way to accept real card and transfer payments today.

Not sure which option fits your business? Message us on WhatsApp — happy to walk you through both.

Where it starts falling short

A payment link has no product catalogue, no branding of your own, and no memory of what someone was buying — each sale is a one-off, manually created link. Once you're selling more than a handful of products or fielding orders at real volume, generating a fresh link for every sale becomes its own full-time job, and customers never get to browse — they have to already know exactly what they want.

When a real website checkout earns its cost

Once you have a real product range, a website ties the catalogue, the price, and the payment into one branded experience — the customer browses, picks, and pays in one place, under your name, not a generic payment page. It also means your site can keep selling while you're asleep, without you manually creating a single link.

Neither option is "wrong." A payment link is a legitimate way to start. A real website checkout is what you grow into once manually creating links starts costing you more time than it saves.
