Hidden Costs of a "Cheap" Website Nobody Warns You About

By Jonabby & Co. Enterprises · October 9, 2026

The upfront price on a cheap website is rarely the full story. A lot of business owners pay a low number upfront, feel like they got a bargain, and then discover the real cost in pieces over the following year. Here's what to watch for.

Hosting and domain renewal, charged separately

Some cheap quotes cover only the build — hosting and the domain name are "not included," and you discover this when the first renewal invoice arrives, often from a provider you've never dealt with directly, at whatever price they choose to charge. Always ask explicitly: is hosting and domain included, and for how long?

Every small change costs extra

Changing a phone number, updating a price, swapping a photo — with some cheap setups, every single edit comes with its own invoice, because there was never a plan for you to make basic updates yourself or get occasional support included. Those small charges add up fast over a year.

Rebuilding when it breaks

Cheaper builds sometimes cut corners on the underlying code — work that looks fine at launch but breaks when a browser updates, or simply stops working with no one available to fix it. At that point, you're not paying to maintain a website anymore; you're paying to rebuild one from scratch, on top of what you already spent.

Lost business while it's down or broken

This is the cost that's hardest to put a number on but often the biggest: every day a broken contact form sits unnoticed, or a site is slow enough that visitors leave before it loads, is a customer who went to a competitor instead. A cheap site that doesn't quite work is, in a real sense, more expensive than a solid one that does.

How to protect yourself upfront

Before you pay anyone, ask directly: what's included after launch? Who pays for hosting and the domain, and when does that renew? What happens if something breaks? A trustworthy answer is specific and doesn't dodge the question — if the answer is vague, assume the hidden costs will show up later.

None of this means cheap is always wrong. It means the real price of a website is what you'll have spent twelve months from now — not just what you paid on day one.
