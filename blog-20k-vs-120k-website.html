₦20k Website vs ₦120k Website: What's the Real Difference?

By Jonabby & Co. Enterprises · October 9, 2026

Both quotes say "website." Both will technically get something live on the internet. But a ₦20,000 site and a ₦120,000 site are rarely the same product at different prices — they're usually genuinely different products. Here's a head-to-head look at what that ₦100,000 gap actually buys.

Pages and structure

₦20,000 typically buys a single page with anchor links (one long scrolling page, sections linked from a menu). ₦120,000 usually buys 3–5 separate pages — a real Home, About, Services, Gallery, and Contact, each its own URL. That matters for Google: more real pages means more chances to show up in search results for different things people search.

Design: template vs custom

At the lower price point, you're almost always getting a template with your logo and colours swapped in — fast to produce, but generic. At ₦120,000, design time is actually spent on your brand specifically: your photos, your layout decisions, your customer's actual buying journey considered from the start.

Features that actually drive business

A photo gallery that doesn't just dump images in a grid but actually showcases your best work. A contact form that emails you directly instead of just a WhatsApp link. Google Maps embedded so customers can find you without leaving the page. These are the kind of details that get left out at the cheapest price point, not because they're hard, but because they take time nobody's being paid for at ₦20,000.

Support after launch

A ₦20,000 site is often a one-time transaction — paid, delivered, done, with no real relationship afterward. A ₦120,000 project usually comes with actual guidance: help with hosting setup, a point of contact when something needs changing, and someone who still picks up the phone six months later.

So which one should you choose?

If you're testing a brand-new, unproven business idea, the cheaper option might be the right call for now. But if you're an established business whose website is meant to actually bring in customers — not just exist — the gap between ₦20,000 and ₦120,000 is usually the gap between a website and a website that works.

The real question isn't which price is lower. It's which one you'll still be happy with in a year.
