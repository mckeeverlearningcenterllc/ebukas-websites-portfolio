Google Business Profile vs a Website: Do You Need Both?

By Jonabby & Co. Enterprises · October 14, 2026 · 2 min read

Google Business Profile is free, takes twenty minutes to set up, and gets your business showing up on Google Maps with your hours, photos, and reviews. Given all that, a fair question follows: if it's free and it works, why pay for a website too?

What Google Business Profile is actually for

It's built for one specific moment: someone nearby, searching for exactly what you offer, right now. "Pharmacy near me," "salon open now" — these map-and-listings searches are where Google Business Profile shines, showing your location, hours, and reviews directly in search results without anyone needing to click through anywhere.

Where it runs out of room

A Business Profile gives you a few photos, a short description, and a list of basic details — there's no space to properly explain your services, show a full portfolio, display real pricing, or tell your story in a way that actually builds trust. It's a listing, not a presentation of your business.

You don't fully control it

Google can and does change how business profiles display, what fields are required, and how reviews are weighted — all without asking you. Your website is the one piece of your online presence Google can't alter, restrict, or de-emphasize on a whim.

It doesn't help people who aren't searching locally

A Business Profile is tied to "near me" style local search. Someone who finds you through a shared link, a social media post, or a referral from a friend doesn't go through Google Maps at all — they need somewhere real to land and learn about you, which only a website provides.

The combination that actually works

Set up your Google Business Profile — it's free, and skipping it means losing every "near me" search to a competitor who bothered to claim theirs. Then link it straight to a real website, so anyone who clicks through from Maps lands somewhere that actually sells them on your business, not just confirms your opening hours.

This isn't an either-or decision. Google Business Profile wins you the local search moment; your website wins the customer once they've found you. Skipping either one leaves money on the table.
